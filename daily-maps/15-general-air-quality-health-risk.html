AirWatch: General Air Quality Health Risk

How to read this map
Primary signal: modeled current U.S. AQI at each state centroid from Open-Meteo/CAMS.
Hazard overlay: active NWS air-quality, smoke, dust, and ash products.
Exposure: CDC/ATSDR 2022 state population.
Consequence / vulnerability: population-weighted CDC/ATSDR overall SVI.

AQI values are modeled screening estimates, not regulatory monitor observations. NWS products remain official products. The MitchellCo attention score is derived.
Where is current modeled air quality most concerning after accounting for population, vulnerability, and active NWS air-health products?

Derived attention score
aqiHazard = min(60, 0.40 × U.S. AQI)
exposure = clamp(0, 15, 5 × (log10(statePopulation) − 4))
vulnerability = 15 × populationWeightedSVI
alertBonus = min(10, maximumNwsEventWeight / 4.5)
attention = aqiHazard + exposure + vulnerability + alertBonus, capped 100
AQI is sampled at one representative state centroid, so it does not characterize within-state variation. Active NWS alerts add a limited bonus rather than replacing the continuous AQI baseline.
Reliability & performance
Open-Meteo supports multiple coordinates in one request, so the nationwide baseline uses one bounded request for state centroids. CDC uses one grouped statistics request for all states. Only normalized per-state population is stored in localStorage for 15 days. NWS zones use IndexedDB because geometries are comparatively static. All four paths fail independently, refresh is hidden/offline aware, and routine refresh does not reset map position.
Sources & attribution
Open-Meteo Air Quality API: current U.S. AQI, PM2.5, PM10 and ozone, using CAMS atmospheric composition forecasts. Data attribution: Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) and Open-Meteo.

National Weather Service API: active air-quality, smoke, dust and ash alert products.

CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 county layer: population and overall SVI percentile aggregated to state.
